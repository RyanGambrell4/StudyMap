How this blog is written

StudyEdge AI is a study planning app. This blog exists because the same questions come up constantly from the students who use it: how to revise so it sticks, how to plan a term, what the research actually says about the techniques everyone recommends.

Articles are written and maintained by StudyEdge AI. There is no separate newsroom and no panel of outside reviewers. When we describe a study, we link straight to the original paper so you can check it rather than take our word for it.

How we handle research

  • Sources are linked, not just named. Where an article makes a claim that rests on published research, it links to that paper directly, usually by DOI.
  • Uncertain findings are described as uncertain. Parts of learning science are genuinely contested. Where the evidence is mixed, the article says so instead of presenting one result as settled.
  • Advice has to be usable. A finding earns a place only if it changes what a student would actually do on a given evening.
  • We say when StudyEdge AI is the answer, and when it is not. The product is mentioned where it is genuinely relevant. Several articles recommend methods and tools that have nothing to do with it.
  • Dates are real. The date on an article is when it was last meaningfully revised, not an automatically refreshed timestamp.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, out of date, or misrepresents a study, tell us and we will fix it: support@getstudyedge.com. Corrections to factual claims are made in place and the revision date is updated.
