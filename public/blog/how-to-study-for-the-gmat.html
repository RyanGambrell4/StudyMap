How to Study for GMAT

The GMAT is the longest-standing MBA admissions test. The current GMAT has three sections: Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, and Data Insights. Here is how to plan a 3- to 6-month prep cycle and which patterns actually move your score.

Key Takeaways
  • GMAT Quant tests logical reasoning more than advanced math -- most content is at 8th-10th grade math level applied to tricky setups
  • Verbal (Critical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension) rewards structured reasoning -- identify premise, conclusion, and assumption before answering
  • Integrated Reasoning is machine-scored and often neglected; a strong IR score signals analytical ability that business schools value
  • The official GMAC prep materials (OG, OG Verbal, OG Quant) are the most accurate indicators of actual exam questions -- prioritize these over third-party books
  • Accuracy on harder questions matters more than finishing all questions; aggressive time management on easy questions frees more time for harder ones

How the current GMAT is structured

GMAC, which runs the exam, now calls it simply the current version of the GMAT. It has three sections, and you choose the order:

  • Quantitative Reasoning: 21 questions in 45 minutes
  • Verbal Reasoning: 23 questions in 45 minutes
  • Data Insights: 20 questions in 45 minutes

That is 64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes, with one optional 10-minute break after the first or second section (mba.com, exam structure). If a prep book describes a different format, it was written for an older version of the exam; plan around the current one.

Total scores run from 205 to 805 in steps of 10, and the three sections count equally. Each section is scored from 60 to 90. Scores are valid for five years, and you can take the exam up to five times in a rolling 12-month period, with at least 16 days between attempts (GMAT scores, exam policies).

Quantitative Reasoning

GMAT quant rewards problem framing more than calculation. With 45 minutes for 21 questions you have a little over two minutes each, so practice setting a problem up quickly rather than grinding through arithmetic. Data Sufficiency questions, which ask whether you have enough information to answer rather than for the answer itself, are unlike anything most people saw in college. Drill them in focused blocks until the format feels routine.

Verbal: critical reasoning and reading comp

Critical Reasoning is essentially LSAT logical reasoning with a business twist. Many of the strategies transfer. Reading Comprehension uses dense passages where extracting structure beats reading every word.

Data Insights

Data Insights asks you to read and combine information from charts, tables and passages, 20 questions in 45 minutes. Because the section is newer than most prep books, use GMAC's official practice for the closest match to the real thing.

Use official practice exhaustively

GMAC writes the exam, so its Official Guide problems and official practice exams are the closest match to the real test's style and difficulty. Third-party books and courses are useful for explanations and extra drills; use the official material to check where you stand.

Build a study plan around your test date

This guide uses a 12-week plan; give yourself longer if your first official practice score is far from your target. A study schedule generator can lay out the week-by-week structure automatically from your test date. Within the plan: weeks 1 to 4 on content review and easy drilling, weeks 5 to 8 on focused weakness work, weeks 9 to 11 on timed sections and full tests, week 12 on test prep and rest.

Score plateaus

When practice scores stall, the fix is usually diagnosis, not more volume: find the question subtypes you keep missing. Pull every wrong answer from your last 3 tests, tag by subtype, and grind the worst three subtypes for two weeks.

GMAT score improvement is not about doing more practice. It is about doing targeted practice on the specific question types and reasoning gaps where you are currently losing points.

Test day

Sleep matters. Avoid new techniques late in prep. Take your last full test 4 to 7 days before. Light review only in the final 2 days. Eat the same breakfast you ate before your best practice tests. Trust the prep.

The bottom line on GMAT prep

GMAT performance is about practice quality more than practice volume. Students who take practice tests without reviewing every wrong answer in detail are wasting the most valuable part of the process. The wrong answers reveal the specific reasoning gaps and timing patterns that are holding the score below target. Identifying those patterns and fixing them specifically, rather than doing more practice of the same kind, is what moves the score.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to prepare for the GMAT?

It depends on how far your starting score is from your target. Take an official GMAT practice exam first: it shows which sections need the most work and how long to plan for. Weekly full-length practice tests combined with focused review of your weakest question types is a sound structure.

What is a good GMAT score for business school?

It depends on the program. Business schools publish the median or average GMAT score of their incoming class, so check each school you are applying to and aim at or above its figure. The current exam is scored from 205 to 805, so compare against figures reported for the current version.

How is the GMAT structured?

The current GMAT has three sections, taken in any order you choose: Quantitative Reasoning (21 questions, 45 minutes), Verbal Reasoning (23 questions, 45 minutes) and Data Insights (20 questions, 45 minutes). That is 64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes, with one optional 10-minute break. Total scores run from 205 to 805, and each section is scored from 60 to 90, according to mba.com.

Is the GMAT or GRE better for MBA applications?

Many MBA programs accept both. Check each program's admissions page for which tests it takes and the scores of its last class, then take a free official practice test for each exam and compare your starting points before you commit.

What GMAT prep resources are most effective?

Start with GMAC's own material: the official practice exams on mba.com and the Official Guide, because GMAC writes the real test. Third-party books and courses are useful for explanations and extra drills. Whatever you use, track your error types across practice sessions so your remaining time goes to the questions you actually miss.
