Grade points use the common 4.0 scale (A = 4.0, A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3). Some schools differ, and the calculator assumes new courses add to your record rather than replacing old grades.

How the math works

Your cumulative GPA is total grade points divided by total credits. If you have a GPA of G over C credits and you add n courses of c credits each at grade g, your new GPA is (G × C + g × c × n) / (C + c × n). Setting that equal to your target T and solving for n gives:

Courses needed = C × (T - G) / (c × (g - T)), rounded up.

Two things fall straight out of the formula. The more credits you already have (C), the more courses each tenth of a point costs. And if the grade you expect is at or below your target (g ≤ T), no number of courses gets you there.

What actually moves a GPA faster

Because the math gets harder every semester, the earliest semesters matter most. If your school offers grade replacement for retaken courses, retaking a low grade can move your GPA more than a new A, since it removes the old grade points instead of only diluting them. Policies differ a lot, so read your registrar's repeat policy before planning around it.

Questions

Can I raise my GPA from 2.5 to 3.0 in one semester?

It depends on how many credits you already have. With 30 credits at a 2.5, you need 15 credits of straight A's to reach exactly 3.0: (2.5 × 30 + 4.0 × 15) / 45 = 3.0. That is five 3-credit courses, which is a full semester for many students. With 60 credits at a 2.5, the same jump takes 30 credits of A's, about two semesters.

Why is it harder to raise my GPA as a senior?

Your GPA is an average over every credit you have taken. Each new grade moves the average less as the total grows. Going from 3.2 to 3.5 takes 6 three-credit A's with 30 credits behind you, but 12 with 60 credits and 18 with 90.

Do retaken classes count differently?

Some schools replace the old grade when you retake a course, and others average both attempts or keep both on the record. This calculator assumes every new course adds to your record. Check your registrar's repeat policy, because grade replacement can raise a GPA faster than new courses can.

Can I get to a 4.0 if I already have a lower grade?

Not unless your school lets a retake replace that grade. Once a grade below an A counts toward your GPA, a perfect 4.0 is out of reach, because new A's can only bring the average closer to 4.0. You can get very close, which the calculator shows.
