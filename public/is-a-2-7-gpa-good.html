Is a 2.7 GPA good?

A 2.7 GPA is a B- average on the standard 4.0 scale. It is below the 3.0 minimum that some graduate programs list, and for jobs it matters mainly where an employer asks for GPA. Here is an honest look at what it means and what you can realistically do about it.

What a 2.7 GPA means

On the University of Chicago grading scale a B- is worth 2.7, so a 2.7 GPA is a B- average; UT Austin counts a B- as 2.67. If a graduate program lists a 3.0 minimum, a 2.7 is below it. For jobs, it matters where an employer asks for GPA. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting.

The most important thing at a 2.7 is to understand what you can still change and what is already locked in. If you have remaining semesters, the math of recovery is worth running. If you have graduated, the strategy shifts to highlighting strengths in your application materials and experience.

Can you get into graduate school with a 2.7 GPA?

It is challenging at programs that list a 3.0 minimum. If a graduate program sets a minimum GPA, it says so on its admissions page. If you are below a stated minimum, your application may not make it past initial screening. Some programs will consider exceptional cases (outstanding test scores, relevant professional experience, or a strong upward trajectory in later coursework), so check each program's policy.

Practical paths forward from a 2.7 if grad school is the goal:

  • Post-baccalaureate coursework: Taking additional college courses and earning high grades can rebuild your academic record. Some programs allow you to apply with a combination of undergraduate and post-bacc GPA.
  • Grade replacement: Some schools allow grade replacement, where retaking a course replaces the original grade in the GPA calculation.
  • Programs with lower stated minimums: Some programs list lower minimums or weigh years of work experience alongside GPA. Check each program's admissions page.
  • Strong GRE/GMAT/LSAT: A very high standardized test score cannot fully compensate for a low GPA, but it can move your application from the "automatic no" to "worth a look" category at some programs.

Is a 2.7 GPA bad for jobs?

A 2.7 is not the career-ender it might feel like. It only matters where an employer asks for GPA. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. Read postings before you assume the worst. Where GPA is not asked for, skills, experience, and interview performance carry the decision.

If a posting you care about lists a minimum above 2.7, a referral or a strong internship record can still get you a conversation, but expect an uphill application.

How to raise a 2.7 GPA

Use the GPA calculator to model exactly how many credits of A-level work it would take to reach 3.0 from your current position. The math is sobering but important: with 90 credits at 2.7, reaching 3.0 takes 27 credits of straight 4.0 work, or 54 credits at a 3.5.

If recovery is still possible, focus on high-credit courses where you are losing the most points. One A in a 4-credit course does more GPA work than raising three 1-credit courses by one letter each. Use active recall and spaced repetition rather than passive review. In one well-known experiment (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, Experiment 2), students read a prose passage. A week later, students who studied it once and took three recall tests remembered 61% of its ideas, against 40% for students who studied it four times. On a test 5 minutes after studying, restudying looked better, which is why re-reading feels like it works (Psychological Science, 2006). Frequently asked questions

Is a 2.7 GPA good?

It is a B- average on the standard 4.0 scale. It does not close every door, but it is below the 3.0 minimum some graduate programs list. For jobs, it matters where an employer asks for GPA; if an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting.

Is a 2.7 GPA passing?

Yes. A 2.7 is above 2.0, the minimum for good academic standing at schools that use that rule (your catalog has your school's number). But passing and competitive are different thresholds: a 2.7 is below any graduate program minimum of 3.0.

Can you get into graduate school with a 2.7 GPA?

It is difficult at programs that list a 3.0 minimum. Some programs consider applicants below their minimum who have strong test scores, work experience or an upward trend. Post-baccalaureate coursework and grade replacement, where your school offers it, can help rebuild the GPA.

Will a 2.7 GPA prevent me from getting a job?

Not for jobs that do not ask for GPA. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. Build skills, experience and a strong interview presence to compensate.

How many As do I need to raise a 2.7 to a 3.0?

It depends on how many credits you have. With 60 credits at 2.7, straight A's (4.0) get you to 3.0 in 18 credits, and a 3.6 average gets you there in 30. With 90 credits at 2.7, it takes 27 credits of straight A's. Use the GPA calculator to model your exact situation.

Can you go to law school with a 2.7 GPA?

Every ABA-accredited law school publishes the GPA percentiles of its entering class in its Standard 509 disclosure at abarequireddisclosures.org. Compare a 2.7 with the 25th percentile GPA at the schools you are considering. The same disclosures list LSAT percentiles, so you can see how much a high LSAT would need to carry.
