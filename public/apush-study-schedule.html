APUSH Study Schedule

The 8-week AP U.S. History plan, built on the Fall 2026 Course and Exam Description. Content weeks follow the published period weights, short-answer practice starts in week 1, document-based and long essay practice build from week 3, and there are two full-length practice exams. Print it, put it on your calendar, or let the AI adapt it.

Exam format (from the Fall 2026 CED): 3 hours 15 minutes in total. Section I is 55 multiple-choice questions in 55 minutes (40% of the score), then 3 required short-answer questions in 40 minutes (20%). Section II is 1 document-based question with seven documents in 60 minutes, including a 15-minute reading period (25%), and 1 required long essay question in 40 minutes (15%). Section I is 60% of the score and Section II is 40%. The 2027 exam is on Friday, May 7, 2027, in the morning session.

The 8-week schedule

WeekFocusWhat to actually do
Week 1
Mar 15		Periods 1 & 2: 1491-1754Native American societies before contact, European exploration, the Columbian Exchange, labor and caste in the Spanish colonial system, the regions of the British colonies, transatlantic trade, slavery in the British colonies, and colonial society. 20 released multiple-choice questions. Combined 10-14% of the exam. Write 1 short-answer question to learn the format.
Week 2
Mar 22		Periods 3 & 4: 1754-1848The Seven Years' War, taxation without representation, the Revolution, the Articles of Confederation, the Constitution and ratification debates, the early republic, the rise of political parties, the market revolution, Jacksonian democracy, the Second Great Awakening and reform movements. 10-17% each. 1 timed short-answer set (3 questions, 40 minutes).
Week 3
Mar 29		Periods 5 & 6: 1844-1898Manifest Destiny, the Mexican-American War, the Compromise of 1850, sectional conflict and secession, the Civil War, Reconstruction and its failure, then westward expansion, the "New South," industrial capitalism, labor, immigration and politics in the Gilded Age. 10-17% each. 1 short-answer question and 1 document-based question (untimed, learning the format).
Week 4
Apr 5		Period 7: 1890-1945Imperialism and the Spanish-American War, the Progressives, World War I at home and abroad, the 1920s, the Great Depression, the New Deal, World War II mobilization and postwar diplomacy. 10-17%. 2 timed short-answer questions and 1 timed document-based question.
Week 5
Apr 12		Periods 8 & 9: 1945-presentThe Cold War, the Red Scare, the postwar economy and culture, the civil rights movements, Vietnam, the Great Society, the environment, then Reagan and conservatism, the end of the Cold War, the changing economy, migration, and challenges of the 21st century. 10-17% and 4-6%. Take Practice Exam 1 at the end of the week under real timing.
Week 6
Apr 19		Document-based question intensiveWrite 2 timed document-based questions. Each one needs: a thesis with a line of reasoning, contextualization (broader developments before, during or after the prompt's time frame), content from at least four documents used to support your argument, one piece of specific evidence beyond the documents, sourcing explained for at least two documents (HAPP: historical situation, audience, purpose, point of view), and a complex understanding of the topic. The DBQ topic falls between 1754 and 1980, so Periods 3-8 are the ones to review this week.
Week 7
Apr 26		Long essay question + Practice Exam 2Write 2 timed long essays (40 minutes each) on prompts from different parts of the course. The long essay is required, covers about half of the course's chronological scope, and you can focus on one or more developments named in the prompt. Take Practice Exam 2. Compare it with Practice Exam 1 and target weak periods and weak question types.
Week 8
May 3		Sharpen & restShort spaced review across all 9 periods. 2 short-answer questions per day Mon-Wed. Thursday: timeline flashcards and thesis practice, then sleep. The exam is Friday morning, May 7.

Period weights on the APUSH exam

The Fall 2026 CED publishes these weights for the nine units, one per period. Use them to give more review time to higher-weighted periods.

  • Unit 1: Period 1 (1491-1607): 4-6%
  • Unit 2: Period 2 (1607-1754): 6-8%
  • Unit 3: Period 3 (1754-1800): 10-17%
  • Unit 4: Period 4 (1800-1848): 10-17%
  • Unit 5: Period 5 (1844-1877): 10-17%
  • Unit 6: Period 6 (1865-1898): 10-17%
  • Unit 7: Period 7 (1890-1945): 10-17%
  • Unit 8: Period 8 (1945-1980): 10-17%
  • Unit 9: Period 9 (1980-present): 4-6%

The 4-week compressed version

  • Week 1: Periods 1-4. Skim Periods 1 and 2, spend most sessions on 3 and 4. 20 multiple-choice questions per period pair.
  • Week 2: Periods 5-7. Two short-answer questions. Practice Exam 1.
  • Week 3: Periods 8-9 and a document-based question intensive. Practice Exam 2.
  • Week 4: Weak-area targeting Mon-Wed. Long essay practice Thursday. Rest before the exam.

What most APUSH students get wrong

They over-invest in Period 1 and Period 9 (combined weight only 8-12%) and under-invest in Periods 3-8 (at least 60% combined, since each is 10-17%). Follow the weights.

They memorize dates and neglect themes. The CED lists eight course themes: American and National Identity; Work, Exchange, and Technology; Geography and the Environment; Migration and Settlement; Politics and Power; America in the World; American and Regional Culture; and Social Structures. The document-based and long essay questions are scored on argument and evidence, not date recall.

They skip long essay practice because "I already do DBQs." The two essays share a rubric structure but not the same task: the long essay gives you no documents, so all of your evidence has to come from what you know. Write at least three before exam day.
