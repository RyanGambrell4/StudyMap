AP Physics 1 Study Schedule

The 8-week plan for AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based, built on the eight-unit course framework the College Board introduced for Fall 2024. It gives a full week each to Force and Translational Dynamics and to Work, Energy, and Power (18-23% of the multiple-choice section each), covers rotation and fluids, and drills all four free-response question types before two full practice exams.

The course changed in 2024-25. The AP Physics 1 Course and Exam Description, effective Fall 2024, has eight units. Circular motion and gravitation are now topics inside Unit 2, rotation has two units of its own (5 and 6), fluids are Unit 8, and mechanical waves are no longer part of AP Physics 1. If a review book has a "Circular Motion and Gravitation" unit or a waves chapter, it is using the old framework.

Exam format (from the CED): 3 hours in total. Section I is 42 multiple-choice questions in 85 minutes, worth 50% of the score. Section II is 4 free-response questions in 95 minutes, worth 50%, in this order: Mathematical Routines (10 points), Translation Between Representations (12 points), Experimental Design and Analysis (10 points) and Qualitative/Quantitative Translation (8 points). A four-function, scientific or graphing calculator is allowed on both sections, and you get the AP Physics 1 equation sheet. The course is algebra-based, so no calculus is required. The 2027 exam is on Wednesday, May 5, 2027, in the afternoon session.

The 8-week schedule

WeekFocusWhat to actually do
Week 1Unit 1: KinematicsScalars and vectors, displacement, velocity and acceleration, the constant-acceleration equations (v = v₀ + at, x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at², v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)), reference frames and relative motion, and motion in two dimensions. Projectile motion has zero acceleration in one dimension and constant acceleration in the other, so treat the components separately. Read position, velocity and acceleration graphs: slope of position-time is velocity, slope of velocity-time is acceleration, and area under velocity-time is displacement. 20 multiple-choice questions.
Week 2Unit 2: Force and Translational DynamicsSystems and center of mass, free-body diagrams, Newton's three laws (Fnet = ma), gravitational force and apparent weight, static and kinetic friction (static friction takes whatever value prevents slipping, up to μsN), spring forces, and circular motion (centripetal acceleration ac = v²/r is the component of acceleration toward the center; it is not a separate force). Circular orbits use Kepler's third law; the first and second laws are outside the course. Draw the free-body diagram before every equation. 25 problems.
Week 3Unit 3: Work, Energy, and PowerTranslational kinetic energy (K = ½mv²), work as energy transferred by a force exerted over a distance, gravitational and spring potential energy, conservation of energy, and power as the rate of energy change. A single-object system can only have kinetic energy, so define the system before you count potential energy. Use energy bar charts to check your setup. For every problem, decide between kinematics and energy before calculating. 25 problems and 1 timed Mathematical Routines question.
Week 4Unit 4: Linear Momentum, then Unit 5 beginsMomentum (p = mv), impulse (average force times time interval, equal to the change in momentum), conservation of momentum when the net external force is zero, and elastic versus inelastic collisions (only elastic collisions keep the system's kinetic energy constant). Then start Unit 5: rotational kinematics (angular displacement, velocity and acceleration), connecting linear and rotational motion (v = rω), and torque, where the lever arm is the perpendicular distance from the axis to the force's line of action. 20 problems and 1 timed Translation Between Representations question.
Week 5Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics, and Unit 6: Energy and Momentum of Rotating SystemsRotational inertia (smallest when the axis passes through the center of mass), rotational equilibrium, and Newton's second law in rotational form (net torque changes angular velocity). Then rotational kinetic energy (½Iω²), work done by torque, angular momentum and angular impulse, conservation of angular momentum when net external torque is zero, rolling (total kinetic energy is translational plus rotational) and orbiting satellites. Take Practice Exam 1 at the end of the week under real timing: 85 minutes for 42 multiple-choice questions, then 95 minutes for 4 free-response questions.
Week 6Unit 7: Oscillations, and Unit 8: FluidsSimple harmonic motion happens when the restoring force is proportional to displacement from equilibrium. Period and frequency (T = 1/f), the period of a mass on a spring and of a pendulum at small angles, why amplitude does not change the period, and energy in an oscillator. Then fluids: density, pressure as force per unit area, absolute and gauge pressure, buoyant force (equal to the weight of the fluid displaced), the continuity equation (A₁v₁ = A₂v₂) and Bernoulli's equation. Redo every problem you missed on Practice Exam 1.
Week 7Free-response week, then Practice Exam 2Write one of each question type, in exam order, every session. Mathematical Routines: derive symbolically, calculate, and justify a claim with physics principles. Translation Between Representations: draw the diagram, write the equations and sketch the graph, then show that they agree. Experimental Design and Analysis: describe a procedure a high school lab could run, varying one quantity and measuring one, then graph given data and use the slope or intercept. Qualitative/Quantitative Translation: make and justify a claim in words, derive an equation, and check that the two agree. Take Practice Exam 2 at the end of the week.
Week 8Weak-unit review and restCompare both practice exams by unit and by question type. Mon-Thu: 2 free-response questions a day on your two weakest units, plus 15 multiple-choice questions mixed across all eight units. Read through the equation sheet so you know what is on it and what is not; the CED marks some relationships as derived, so practice deriving them. Rest the day before the exam. Bring pencils and an approved calculator.

Unit weights on the AP Physics 1 exam

The CED publishes these weights for the multiple-choice section. Units 2 and 3 are the two largest, and Units 1 to 4 together (translational mechanics) are 56-76% of the section by the published ranges.

  • Unit 1: Kinematics: 10-15%
  • Unit 2: Force and Translational Dynamics: 18-23% (includes circular motion and gravitational force)
  • Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power: 18-23%
  • Unit 4: Linear Momentum: 10-15%
  • Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics: 10-15%
  • Unit 6: Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems: 5-8%
  • Unit 7: Oscillations: 5-8%
  • Unit 8: Fluids: 10-15%

The free-response section is weighted by question type, not by unit, and any question can draw on any unit. For quick review of the definitions in each unit, use the AP Physics 1 flashcards, which follow this same framework.

Source: College Board, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Course and Exam Description, Effective Fall 2024 (unit titles, topics, multiple-choice unit weights, exam format and free-response question types). 2027 exam date from the College Board's 2027 AP Exam Schedule.

Where AP Physics 1 students lose points

They study from the old framework. Review books and released exams written before 2024-25 have a different unit list, include waves, and use the older exam format. The physics overlaps, but rotation and fluids now have whole units, and the free-response section has four questions with fixed types.

They draw incomplete free-body diagrams. Common errors are leaving out the normal force on an incline, pointing friction the wrong way, adding a "force of motion" that does not exist, and forgetting that centripetal force is the net inward force rather than an extra arrow. When a question asks you to draw the diagram, the diagram itself is scored.

They reach for kinematics on every problem. Kinematics needs constant acceleration and usually involves time. When the question gives heights, speeds at two points or a spring compression and does not ask about time, energy is often the shorter path. Practice naming the method before you start.

They give numbers without a justification. Several free-response question types ask you to make a claim and support it with physics principles. "4.9 m/s" alone does not answer "explain." Name the principle ("momentum of the system is conserved because the net external force is zero"), set up the equation from it, and say what the result means.

They mix up elastic and inelastic collisions. Momentum is conserved in both when the net external force is zero. Only in an elastic collision is the system's kinetic energy the same before and after. Objects that stick together have had a perfectly inelastic collision.

The 4-week compressed version

  • Week 1: Unit 1 (two sessions) and Unit 2 (three sessions). Free-body diagram on every problem. 15 multiple-choice questions per unit.
  • Week 2: Unit 3 (three sessions) and Unit 4 (two sessions). Practice Exam 1 multiple-choice section at the end of the week under timed conditions.
  • Week 3: Units 5 and 6 (three sessions), then Units 7 and 8 (two sessions). One free-response question of each type across the week.
  • Week 4: Full timed practice exam early in the week. Score it by unit and question type, drill your weakest areas Mon-Thu, and rest before exam day.
