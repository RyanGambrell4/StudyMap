Is a 3.2 GPA good?

A 3.2 GPA sits between a B (3.0) and a B+ (3.3) on the standard 4.0 scale. It meets any graduate program minimum of 3.0. It is also below the median at most ABA law schools. Here is what it actually means, where it falls short, and what to do about it.

What a 3.2 GPA means

On the University of Chicago grading scale a B is 3.0 and a B+ is 3.3, so a 3.2 sits between them, closer to a B+. UT Austin counts a B+ as 3.33. A 3.2 does not carry the same weight as a 3.5 or 3.7, but it is not a GPA that closes major doors either. Grading differs between schools and majors, so the most useful comparison is local: check whether your registrar or department publishes grade distributions.

The thresholds worth knowing are the ones your targets publish: the minimum a program lists, the median GPA it reports for admitted students, and any cutoff an employer states in a posting.

Is a 3.2 GPA good for graduate school?

A 3.2 meets the minimum at programs that list 3.0. If a graduate program sets a minimum GPA, it says so on its admissions page. Here is how it plays out in major categories:

  • Master's programs: A 3.2 meets a 3.0 minimum. At competitive programs, strong test scores, research experience, or a compelling statement of purpose can help close the gap to the program's reported median.
  • Law school: T14 law schools have medians of 3.88 to 3.99, and a 3.2 is below the 25th percentile at all of them (the lowest is 3.74 at Michigan Law). Across all 196 ABA schools, half report a median between 3.50 and 3.81, so a 3.2 is below the median at more than three quarters of them. Schools whose medians sit closer to a 3.2 are the realistic targets, and a high LSAT carries extra weight.
  • MBA programs: Each program publishes a class profile with the GPA of its entering class. Compare your GPA with those figures; GMAT or GRE scores, work experience and essays carry weight alongside it.
  • Medical school: Medical schools report the GPAs of their entering classes. Compare your GPA with those figures at each MD and DO program you are considering; the MCAT and clinical experience carry weight alongside it.

Law school figures: ABA Standard 509 disclosures, 2025 entering class (abarequireddisclosures.org).

Is a 3.2 GPA good for jobs?

A 3.2 matters for a job only where the employer asks for GPA. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. If a posting you want lists a higher minimum, referrals and networking can get you a conversation.

Where GPA is not part of the application, a 3.2 combined with relevant internships, strong technical skills, and solid interviews is enough to compete.

What to do if you have a 3.2 GPA

If you have remaining semesters, use the grade calculator to find exactly what you need in each current course, then model the cumulative impact with the GPA calculator. Focus effort on high-credit courses. Moving a 4-credit course from B to A lifts your GPA more than improving a 1-credit elective by two letter grades.

Switch from passive re-reading to active recall: practice problems, self-quizzing, and spaced repetition. In one well-known experiment (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, Experiment 2), students read a prose passage. A week later, students who studied it once and took three recall tests remembered 61% of its ideas, against 40% for students who studied it four times. On a test 5 minutes after studying, restudying looked better, which is why re-reading feels like it works (Psychological Science, 2006).

Frequently asked questions

Is a 3.2 GPA good?

It is a solid GPA between a B (3.0) and a B+ (3.3). It meets any graduate program minimum of 3.0. For selective programs, compare it with the GPAs they report for admitted students; at law schools, it is below the median at more than three quarters of ABA schools.

Is a 3.2 GPA good for grad school?

A 3.2 exceeds a 3.0 minimum and qualifies at programs that list one. It is below the median at more than three quarters of ABA-accredited law schools: half of all 196 report a median between 3.50 and 3.81. For MBA and medical programs, compare it with the GPAs each school reports for its entering class. Strong LSAT, GMAT, GRE or MCAT scores matter more at a 3.2.

Is a 3.2 GPA good enough to get a job?

Yes, wherever GPA is not part of the application. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting.

What letter grade is a 3.2 GPA?

It falls between a B and a B+. On the University of Chicago's scale a B is 3.0 and a B+ is 3.3; UT Austin counts a B+ as 3.33.

Is a 3.2 GPA good for nursing school?

Nursing programs set their own GPA requirements, and some look at a separate prerequisite GPA as well as your overall GPA. Check each program's stated minimum and how it calculates the GPA it uses.

How can I raise a 3.2 GPA to a 3.5?
