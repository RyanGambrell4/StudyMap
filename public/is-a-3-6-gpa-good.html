Is a 3.6 GPA good?

A 3.6 GPA is just under an A- (3.7) on the standard 4.0 scale. It clears any employer cutoff set at 3.5. It also sits close to the middle of ABA law school medians. Here is what it means, where it gets you, and whether pushing higher is worth it.

What a 3.6 GPA means

On the University of Chicago grading scale a B+ is 3.3 and an A- is 3.7, so a 3.6 sits just under an A-. UT Austin counts an A- as 3.67. In most contexts, a 3.6 is a genuine academic achievement. Grading differs between schools and majors, so the most useful comparison is local: check whether your registrar or department publishes grade distributions.

Is a 3.6 GPA good for graduate school?

A 3.6 exceeds any stated minimum of 3.0 or 3.5. If a graduate program sets a minimum GPA, it says so on its admissions page. Here is how it maps to major paths:

  • Master's programs: A 3.6 clears any minimum of 3.0 or 3.5. Compare it with the median each program reports to see how competitive it is there.
  • Law school: T14 law schools have median GPAs of 3.88 to 3.99, and a 3.6 is below the 25th percentile at all of them (the lowest is 3.74 at Michigan Law), so a T14 application leans heavily on the LSAT; T14 LSAT medians run 170 to 174. Across all 196 ABA schools, the median of school medians is 3.67, so a 3.6 sits close to the middle of the national range.
  • MBA programs: Each program publishes a class profile with the GPA of its entering class. Compare your GPA with those figures; GMAT or GRE scores, work experience and essays carry weight alongside it.
  • Medical school: Medical schools report the GPAs of their entering classes. Compare your GPA with those figures at each MD and DO program you are considering; the MCAT and clinical experience carry weight alongside it.
  • PhD programs: A 3.6 is a strong GPA for a PhD application, and research experience and recommendations weigh heavily too.

Law school figures: ABA Standard 509 disclosures, 2025 entering class (abarequireddisclosures.org).

Does a 3.6 GPA earn Latin honors?

Each school sets its own Latin honors rules (some by GPA, some by class rank); check your registrar's page. Whether a 3.6 qualifies depends entirely on those rules, so read your school's policy rather than relying on a rule of thumb.

Is a 3.6 GPA good for jobs?

A 3.6 clears any employer GPA cutoff set at 3.5 or lower. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. So read the postings you care about rather than guessing.

Where GPA is not part of the application, interview performance, relevant skills, and experience carry the decision.

Should you try to push your 3.6 higher?

It depends on your target. If a program you are aiming for reports a median of 3.7 or 3.8, a higher GPA moves you closer to it. T14 law school medians run higher still, from 3.88 to 3.99. Calculate with the GPA calculator to see how many credits of A-level work it would actually take to move from 3.6 to 3.7. With 90 credits at a 3.6, it takes 30 credits of straight 4.0 work to reach a 3.7, so weigh that against what else the time could do.

For most students at 3.6, additional time is better invested in LSAT/GMAT/GRE prep, research experience, internships, or essays than in marginal GPA improvement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 3.6 GPA good?

Yes. It is just under an A- (3.7) on the standard 4.0 scale. It clears any employer cutoff set at 3.5 and any graduate program minimum of 3.0. T14 law schools report median GPAs of 3.88 to 3.99.

Is a 3.6 GPA good enough for law school?

A 3.6 sits close to the national middle: across all 196 ABA-accredited law schools, the median of school medians is 3.67. For T14 schools, where medians run from 3.88 to 3.99, a 3.6 is below the 25th percentile at every school. It does not disqualify you, but it puts the weight on a high LSAT (T14 LSAT medians run 170 to 174).

Is a 3.6 GPA good enough for medical school?

Medical schools report the GPAs of their entering classes. Compare a 3.6 with those figures at each MD and DO program you are considering; a strong MCAT score and clinical experience strengthen the application.

Is a 3.6 GPA considered honors?

It depends on your school. Each school sets its own Latin honors rules (some by GPA, some by class rank); check your registrar's page.

How hard is it to raise a 3.6 GPA to a 3.7?

It requires sustained A-level performance. With 90 credits at a 3.6, 15 credits of straight 4.0 work brings you to about 3.66, and reaching 3.7 takes 30 credits of 4.0 work: 324 plus 120 is 444 grade points over 120 credits. Use the GPA calculator to model your specific situation.

What letter grade is a 3.6 GPA?

On the University of Chicago's scale it falls between a B+ (3.3) and an A- (3.7), much closer to the A-.
