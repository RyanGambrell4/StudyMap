Is a 3.8 GPA good?

A 3.8 GPA sits between an A- (3.7) and an A (4.0) on the standard 4.0 scale. It clears any employer cutoff set at 3.5 or 3.7. It is at or above the median at about three quarters of ABA law schools. Here is what it opens and whether chasing 4.0 is actually worth it.

What a 3.8 GPA means

On the University of Chicago grading scale an A- is 3.7 and an A is 4.0, so a 3.8 sits between them. UT Austin counts an A- as 3.67. It signals sustained, excellent academic performance across your coursework. A 3.8 meets any GPA minimum set at 3.8 or lower, so the question is less whether your GPA qualifies you and more whether the rest of your application can match it.

Is a 3.8 GPA good for medical school?

Medical schools report the GPAs of their entering classes, and those figures are the right comparison for a 3.8, at both MD and DO programs. At this GPA, the MCAT, research, clinical experience and personal statement carry much of the outcome.

Is a 3.8 GPA good for law school?

A 3.8 is at or above the median at about three quarters of the 196 ABA-accredited law schools; about a quarter report a median of 3.81 or higher. The T14 is different. Medians there run from 3.88 (Michigan Law) to 3.99 (University of Virginia School of Law), so a 3.8 is below the median at all 14. At Harvard, Yale, and Stanford, which report medians of 3.96, 3.96 and 3.96, a 3.8 is also below the 25th percentile (3.89, 3.90 and 3.87). At Michigan Law (3.74), Cornell (3.75), Georgetown (3.75), Northwestern (3.76) and Penn Carey Law (3.77), a 3.8 is above the 25th percentile.

At a 3.8, your LSAT score becomes the primary admissions variable, not your GPA. A 3.8 with an LSAT above a school's median is a competitive application there; T14 LSAT medians run from 170 to 174. A 3.8 with a lower LSAT is still competitive at most schools, with the LSAT deciding how selective a school you can reach.

Law school figures: ABA Standard 509 disclosures, 2025 entering class (abarequireddisclosures.org).

Is a 3.8 GPA good for MBA programs?

Each MBA program publishes a class profile with the GPA of its entering class; compare a 3.8 with those figures. Admissions are holistic and weigh work experience, career trajectory, and leadership alongside GPA, so a strong GPA does not guarantee admission.

The remaining variables are GMAT/GRE score, years and quality of work experience, and the strength of your professional story.

Is a 3.8 GPA good for jobs?

A 3.8 clears any employer GPA cutoff set at 3.8 or lower. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. Where GPA is listed on a resume, a 3.8 is an asset.

Should you push from 3.8 to 4.0?

For most students, the answer is no, or at least, not at significant cost to other priorities. Here is the honest cost-benefit:

  • What you gain: A 3.8 already meets any stated GPA minimum at or below 3.8. A 4.0 helps where a program or award sets a cutoff above 3.8, or where your school's honors rules reward it.
  • What it costs: Moving a 3.8 to 4.0 means leaving zero room for error in remaining coursework, which can mean avoiding challenging courses, declining interesting opportunities, or spending significantly more hours studying.
  • When it is worth it: If you are targeting academic honors at a school whose rules reward a GPA above yours (check your registrar's page), applying to an award or program with a stated cutoff above 3.8, or your school's competitive environment makes 4.0 meaningful, the push may be worth it. Otherwise, redirect that effort toward research, meaningful extracurriculars, or professional experience.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 3.8 GPA considered excellent?

Yes. It sits between an A- (3.7) and an A (4.0) on the standard 4.0 scale and meets any stated GPA minimum at or below 3.8.

Is a 3.8 GPA good for law school?

For most law schools, yes. A 3.8 is at or above the median at about three quarters of the 196 ABA-accredited schools; about a quarter report a median of 3.81 or higher. It is below the median at every T14 school, where medians run from 3.88 to 3.99, and below the 25th percentile at Harvard, Yale and Stanford. In a T14 application, the LSAT carries much of the weight.

Is a 3.8 GPA good for medical school?

Medical schools, including highly selective ones, report the GPAs of their entering classes. Compare a 3.8 with those figures; at this level the MCAT, research and the rest of the application carry much of the weight.

Is a 3.8 good enough for a scholarship?

Scholarships set their own GPA requirements. A 3.8 meets any stated minimum at or below 3.8; check each award's terms, since competitive awards weigh other qualifications too.

What is the difference between 3.8 and 4.0 in practice?

A 4.0 matters where a program, award or honors rule sets a cutoff above 3.8. Check your school's honors rules on the registrar's page and the terms of anything you plan to apply for.

How many semesters does it take to raise a GPA to 3.8?

It depends entirely on your starting GPA and credit load. With 60 credits at a 3.5, reaching 3.8 takes 90 more credits of straight 4.0 work, about six 15-credit semesters: 210 plus 360 is 570 grade points over 150 credits. Use the cumulative GPA calculator to model your specific situation.
