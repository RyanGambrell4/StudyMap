AP Statistics Study Schedule

An 8-week plan for AP Statistics, built on the five-unit course framework the College Board introduced for Fall 2026. It follows the unit order in the new Course and Exam Description, gives Unit 1 (the largest share of the multiple-choice section) two full weeks, and builds in two practice exams and a final week of free-response writing.

The course changed for 2026-27. The AP Statistics Course and Exam Description, effective Fall 2026, replaces the old nine-unit framework with five units. Probability and sampling distributions now share Unit 2, inference is split into proportions (Unit 3) and means (Unit 4), and regression comes last as Unit 5. If your textbook or an older study guide talks about "Unit 9: Inference for Slopes," it is using the old numbering.

Exam format (from the Fall 2026 CED): 3 hours in total. Section I is 42 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes, worth 50% of the score. Section II is 4 free-response questions in 90 minutes, worth 50%, with each question worth 12.5%. A graphing calculator with statistical capabilities is expected for both sections, and formulas and tables are provided for both sections. The 2027 exam is on Tuesday, May 11, 2027, in the afternoon session.

Unit weights on the AP Statistics exam

The Fall 2026 Course and Exam Description publishes these weights for the multiple-choice section. Unit 1 has the widest share, and Units 2 and 3 together can be up to half of the section.

  • Unit 1: Exploring One-Variable Data and Collecting Data: 20-30% (graphs and summary statistics for one variable, sampling, bias, experimental design)
  • Unit 2: Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions: 15-25% (two categorical variables, probability rules, binomial and normal distributions, sampling distributions and the central limit theorem)
  • Unit 3: Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions: 15-25% (intervals and tests for one and two proportions, chi-square tests for homogeneity and independence)
  • Unit 4: Inference for Quantitative Data: Means: 10-20% (t-intervals and t-tests for one mean, mean differences, and two means)
  • Unit 5: Regression Analysis: 10-20% (scatterplots, correlation, residuals, least-squares regression)

The free-response section is weighted by question rather than by unit: each of the 4 questions is 12.5% of the total score. For quick review of the definitions in each unit, use the AP Statistics flashcards, which are built from this same framework.

What most AP Statistics students get wrong

They write math answers instead of statistics answers. This is the biggest source of lost points on AP Statistics free-response questions. A p-value of 0.04 is not an answer. "Because p = 0.04 < α = 0.05, we reject the null hypothesis. There is convincing evidence that the true proportion of students who prefer the new format is greater than 0.5" is an answer. Every conclusion has to reference what is actually being studied: the variable, the direction and the population.

They skip checking conditions. Checking conditions is part of every inference question. Name each condition, state what it requires, and confirm it is met using numbers from the problem. The College Board's own guidance warns against mixing up the 10% condition with the randomization condition, so keep them separate.

They confuse the paired t-test with the two-sample t-test. If the data come in natural pairs (before and after measurements on the same subject, twins in a study), compute the differences and run a one-sample t procedure on them. If the two groups are independent, use a two-sample t procedure. Picking the wrong procedure costs points even when the arithmetic is right.

They misinterpret confidence intervals. "There is a 95% probability that the true proportion is in this interval" is wrong. The interval either contains the true proportion or it does not. The confidence level describes the method: if we repeated the process many times, about 95% of the intervals built this way would contain the true proportion.

They study from old materials without noticing. Many review books and released exams still use the nine-unit numbering and the previous exam format, which had six free-response questions including an investigative task. The content overlaps a lot, but the current exam has 42 multiple-choice and 4 free-response questions, and inference for regression slopes and the chi-square goodness-of-fit test are not in the Fall 2026 unit topics.

The 4-week compressed version

  • Week 1: Unit 1. Two sessions on graphs and summary statistics, one on comparing distributions and z-scores, two on sampling and experimental design. 15 multiple-choice questions per session.
  • Week 2: Unit 2 and the first half of Unit 3 (inference for one proportion). Check conditions on every problem. Practice Exam 1 at the end of the week.
  • Week 3: The rest of Unit 3 (two proportions and chi-square) and Unit 4 (means). Two complete inference write-ups per session. Practice Exam 2 at the end of the week.
  • Week 4: Unit 5 (regression) Monday and Tuesday, then weak-unit review from both practice exam error logs. Write all inference procedures from memory Thursday. Rest before the exam.

Source: College Board, AP Statistics Course and Exam Description, Effective Fall 2026 (unit titles, topics, multiple-choice unit weights, exam format and inference conditions). 2027 exam date from the College Board's 2027 AP Exam Schedule.
