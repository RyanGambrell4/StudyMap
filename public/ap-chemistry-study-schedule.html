AP Chemistry Study Schedule

The 8-week plan for AP Chemistry, built on the Fall 2024 Course and Exam Description. It gives a full week each to Unit 3 (Properties of Substances and Mixtures, the highest-weighted unit at 18-22%) and Unit 8 (Acids and Bases, 11-15%), and spends the last two weeks on calculation practice, free-response writing and two practice exams.

Exam format (from the Fall 2024 CED): 3 hours 15 minutes in total. Section I is 60 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes, worth 50% of the score. Section II is 7 free-response questions in 105 minutes, worth 50%: 3 long questions (10 points each) and 4 short questions (4 points each). A scientific or graphing calculator is recommended for both sections, and you get a periodic table and a formula sheet. The 2027 exam is on Thursday, May 6, 2027, in the afternoon session.

Unit weights on the AP Chemistry exam

The Fall 2024 CED publishes these weights for the multiple-choice section. Unit 3 alone is 18-22% and Unit 8 is 11-15%; every other unit is 7-9%.

  • Unit 1: Atomic Structure and Properties: 7-9%
  • Unit 2: Compound Structure and Properties: 7-9%
  • Unit 3: Properties of Substances and Mixtures: 18-22% (highest weight)
  • Unit 4: Chemical Reactions: 7-9%

What most AP Chemistry students get wrong

They memorize periodic trends without the reason behind them. The CED explains trends in ionization energy and atomic radius with Coulomb's law, shielding and effective nuclear charge. If you can only state that radius decreases across a period, you cannot answer a question that asks you to explain it.

They avoid the math. Stoichiometry, gas laws, pH and buffers, equilibrium, calorimetry, Gibbs free energy and Faraday's law all involve setting up and solving calculations under time pressure. Every study session should include worked problems, not just reading notes.

They under-prepare for the long free-response questions. The three long questions are worth 10 points each, and they ask you to explain and justify as well as calculate. Practice writing complete responses with full reasoning.

They study topics the exam leaves out, and skip ones it keeps. The CED's exclusion statements say phase diagrams, colligative properties, Arrhenius equation calculations, conversions between Kc and Kp, and electron configurations of aufbau exceptions will not be assessed. Algorithmic Nernst equation calculations are also not the goal: you are expected to reason qualitatively about how concentration changes cell potential. Spend that time on Unit 3 and Unit 8 instead.

They explain entropy vaguely. The CED describes entropy increasing when matter becomes more dispersed (for example, a solid dissolving or more moles of gas forming) or when energy is dispersed. Justify the sign of ΔS with that, using particle counts and phases, not "messiness."

The 4-week compressed version

  • Week 1: Units 1, 2 and 3. Spend at least 3 of 5 sessions on Unit 3 (intermolecular forces, gases, solutions, spectroscopy). 15 multiple-choice questions per unit.
  • Week 2: Units 4, 5, 6 and 7. Prioritize calculation problems: stoichiometry, rate laws, Hess's law, equilibrium. Practice Exam 1 at the end of the week.
  • Week 3: Units 8 and 9. pH, buffer and titration drill (5 problems per session). Gibbs free energy and electrochemistry. Practice Exam 2 at the end of the week. 2 long free-response questions timed.
  • Week 4: Weak-area targeting using both practice exam error logs. Review the formula sheet. Rest the day before the exam on Thursday, May 6.
