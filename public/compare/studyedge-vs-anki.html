The short answer

If you are memorizing a large body of facts over months or years and do not mind building and tuning your own decks, Anki is the stronger flashcard tool: its scheduler is more configurable than ours, its add-ons and shared decks are huge, and it is free everywhere except iOS. If you would rather not make cards by hand and need help deciding what to study across several courses, StudyEdge AI is the better fit.

Choose Anki if

You want the deepest flashcard tool

  • You are memorizing for years (medicine, languages, law)
  • You want to tune the scheduler, including FSRS
  • You want free desktop, Android and web apps
  • You want shared decks and add-ons

Choose StudyEdge AI if

You want cards made for you, plus a plan

  • You do not want to build decks by hand
  • You have several courses with different exam dates
  • You want practice exams as well as flashcards
  • You want to track grades and what you need on the final

When Anki is the better pick

Pick Anki if flashcards are the whole job and the stakes are long term. Medical students, language learners and anyone memorizing for a licensing exam use it because the scheduler is excellent and fully adjustable, including the newer FSRS option, and because the desktop app, Android app and AnkiWeb sync cost nothing.

Its shared decks mean that for some subjects you can start reviewing without making a single card, and add-ons let you change almost anything. Anki's own manual does advise using shared decks as a supplement to your course material for complex subjects.

When StudyEdge AI is the better pick

Pick StudyEdge AI if building cards is the part you never get to. It generates flashcards from your lecture notes and slides, schedules them for review, and also writes practice exams from the same material.

It plans the rest of your studying too: a weekly schedule built from your exam dates across all your courses, a plan for each session, and Grade Hub to show where you stand. Anki's manual does not describe planning around exam dates.

The real difference: building cards or getting them made

Anki gives you a powerful engine and expects you to feed it. You decide what goes on each card, how cards are laid out, and how the scheduler behaves. Done well, that is the most efficient way to memorize a large body of material, which is why it is popular for medicine and languages.

StudyEdge AI makes the opposite trade. It takes your notes and slides and produces the cards for you, with fewer knobs to tune, and puts them inside a plan for the whole semester. You give up some control over the cards in exchange for not having to make them, and for help deciding what to study when.

Can you use both?

Yes. Keep Anki for long-term decks you have built or downloaded, such as a medical or language deck, and use StudyEdge AI to plan your courses, generate cards and practice exams from new lecture material, and track grades. There is no import between them.

Questions

Is Anki free?

Mostly. The desktop app, AnkiWeb and the Android app (AnkiDroid) are free. The iOS app, AnkiMobile, was $24.99 as a one-time purchase on the US App Store on October 30, 2026, usable on up to 5 iOS devices.

Does Anki use AI to make flashcards?

AI card generation is not part of Anki itself; you build cards with note types and templates or download shared decks. StudyEdge AI generates flashcards from your notes, PDFs, slides and recordings.

Which has the better spaced repetition?

Anki's is more configurable: it supports the SM-2 algorithm and the newer FSRS scheduler, with many settings. StudyEdge AI uses SM-2 with fewer settings, and ties review into a study plan built from your exam dates.

Can I import my Anki decks into StudyEdge AI?

No. StudyEdge AI does not import Anki or Quizlet decks. It generates cards from your own course material instead.
