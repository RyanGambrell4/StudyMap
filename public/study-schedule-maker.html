Five Steps

How to make a study schedule

  1. List every course. Include the easy ones. They still need maintenance blocks, just smaller.
  2. Add your exam dates. The next graded event per course: exam, quiz, or major assignment. Exam proximity is the single biggest input to how hours are allocated.
  3. Set your available hours per day. Be honest about weekdays vs weekends. A schedule built on 6 hours you cannot execute is worse than one built on 3 you can.
  4. Weight courses by difficulty. Give each course a 1 to 5 score. Harder courses get more hours and appear earlier in crunch weeks.
  5. Generate the week, execute, iterate. Build the first week, execute it, then rebuild every Sunday based on what you actually got done.
Structure

What a good study schedule looks like

A schedule that survives past week 2 shares five features:

  • Every course appears every week, even in weeks with no exam for that course. Cold restarts are how you fall behind.
  • Blocks are 45 to 90 minutes, not open-ended. Long blocks feel productive and are not.
  • Review is scheduled, not spontaneous. Old material gets a maintenance touch every week.
  • Exam prep starts 10 days before, not 3. The last 72 hours are for review, not learning.
  • One buffer day per week, unallocated. If you fell behind, this is where you catch up.

Common questions about study schedule makers

What is a study schedule maker?

A study schedule maker is a tool that takes your courses, exam dates, and available hours and returns a week-by-week study plan. The best ones balance every course, respect your class times, and front-load harder subjects as exams approach.

How many hours per day should a study schedule include?

Two to four focused hours on weekdays and four to six on weekends for a full course load. A schedule that assumes more than you can execute is worse than one that assumes less.

How often should I remake my study schedule?

Weekly. Rebuild every Sunday based on what you actually got done that week and what is coming next. A schedule that never adjusts fails by week 3.
