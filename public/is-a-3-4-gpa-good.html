Is a 3.4 GPA good?

A 3.4 GPA sits between a B+ (3.3) and an A- (3.7) on the standard 4.0 scale, closer to the B+. It clears any graduate program minimum of 3.0. Here is what it means, where the 3.5 mark matters, and how to use your GPA strategically.

What a 3.4 GPA means

On the University of Chicago grading scale a B+ is 3.3 and an A- is 3.7, so a 3.4 sits between them, closer to the B+. UT Austin counts those grades as 3.33 and 3.67. It represents consistently solid academic performance. Grading differs between schools and majors, so the most useful comparison is local: check whether your registrar or department publishes grade distributions.

The threshold worth checking at a 3.4 is 3.5. If an employer or program you are targeting states a 3.5 minimum, a 3.4 falls just below it, so it is worth knowing which of your targets use that filter and whether the math of raising your GPA works with your remaining credits.

Is a 3.4 GPA good for graduate school?

A 3.4 exceeds any 3.0 minimum. If a graduate program sets a minimum GPA, it says so on its admissions page. Where it stands at selective programs:

  • Master's programs: A 3.4 is solid for master's programs and can be competitive at ranked programs, especially with strong test scores or relevant experience.
  • Law school: T14 medians run 3.88 to 3.99, and T14 LSAT medians run 170 to 174. Across all 196 ABA schools, half report a median GPA between 3.50 and 3.81, so a 3.4 is below the median at more than three quarters of them and a strong LSAT carries more weight.
  • MBA programs: Each program publishes a class profile with the GPA of its entering class. Compare your GPA with those figures; GMAT or GRE scores, work experience and essays carry weight alongside it.
  • Medical school: Medical schools report the GPAs of their entering classes. Compare your GPA with those figures at each MD and DO program you are considering; the MCAT and clinical experience carry weight alongside it.

Law school figures: ABA Standard 509 disclosures, 2025 entering class (abarequireddisclosures.org).

Is a 3.4 GPA good for jobs?

A 3.4 matters for a job only where the employer asks for GPA. If an employer screens on GPA, the cutoff is usually stated in the posting. If a posting you want lists a 3.5 minimum, a 3.4 is just below it.

If those are your targets, it is worth knowing where you stand and whether the math supports pushing your GPA to 3.5 before recruiting season. Use the GPA calculator to model this. If you cannot get to 3.5, networking and referrals can still get you a conversation.

How to push from 3.4 to 3.5

If you want to cross the 3.5 mark, you need consistent A and A- grades in your remaining coursework. With 60 credits at a 3.4, 12 credits of straight A's (4.0) gets you to 3.5; with 90 credits, it takes 18.
