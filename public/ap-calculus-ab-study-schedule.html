AP Calculus AB Study Schedule

The 8-week plan built for AP Calc AB. It follows the eight units in the College Board's Course and Exam Description, gives full weeks to Units 5 and 6 (the two highest-weighted units at 15-20% each), ramps up free-response practice from week 2, and includes two full-length practice exams. Print it, put it on your calendar, or let the AI generator adapt it to your exact date.

Exam format (from the current CED): 3 hours 10 minutes in total. Section I is 42 multiple-choice questions, worth 50% of the score: Part A is 29 questions in 62 minutes with no graphing calculator (35%), and Part B is 13 questions in 38 minutes with a graphing calculator required (15%). Section II is 6 free-response questions, worth 50%: Part A is 2 questions in 30 minutes with a graphing calculator required (16.7%), and Part B is 4 questions in 60 minutes with no calculator (33.3%). The 2027 exam is on Monday, May 10, 2027, in the morning session.

The 8-week schedule

Week Focus What to actually do
Week 1
Mar 15		Unit 1: Limits & ContinuityLimits from graphs, tables and algebraic properties, one-sided limits, the squeeze theorem, types of discontinuities, continuity at a point and over an interval, infinite limits and vertical asymptotes, limits at infinity and horizontal asymptotes, and the Intermediate Value Theorem. Solve 20 released multiple-choice questions. One 45-minute session on why limits underpin everything that follows.
Week 2
Mar 22		Units 2 & 3: DifferentiationAverage and instantaneous rates of change, the definition of the derivative, differentiability and continuity, the power, sum, product and quotient rules, derivatives of trig, exponential and log functions. Then the chain rule, implicit differentiation, derivatives of inverse and inverse trig functions, and higher-order derivatives. 25 practice problems and 1 timed free-response question (calculator-required).
Week 3
Mar 29		Unit 4: Contextual Applications of DifferentiationInterpreting the derivative in context (with units), straight-line motion (position, velocity, acceleration), rates of change in other applied contexts, related rates, local linearity and linearization, and L'Hospital's Rule for 0/0 and ∞/∞. 1 timed free-response question per session, minimum 2 for the week.
Week 4
Apr 5		Unit 5: Analytical Applications of DifferentiationThe Mean Value Theorem, the Extreme Value Theorem and critical points, increasing and decreasing intervals, the first derivative test, the candidates test on closed intervals, concavity, the second derivative test, connecting graphs of f, f′ and f″, and optimization. 15-20% of the multiple-choice section. 2 timed free-response questions, one with a graph of f′.
Week 5
Apr 12		Unit 6: Integration & Accumulation of ChangeAccumulation of change, left, right, midpoint and trapezoidal sums, summation and definite integral notation, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and accumulation functions, properties of definite integrals, antiderivatives and indefinite integrals, and u-substitution (change the limits on definite integrals). 15-20% of the multiple-choice section. 25 practice problems and 1 timed free-response question with a table of values.
Week 6
Apr 19		Units 7 & 8: Differential Equations + Applications of IntegrationModeling with differential equations, verifying solutions, sketching and reasoning with slope fields, separation of variables with initial conditions, and exponential models (dy/dt = ky). Then average value, position, velocity and acceleration with integrals (displacement versus total distance), accumulation in applied contexts, area between curves, and volumes by cross sections, discs and washers. Take Practice Exam 1 at the end of the week under real timing.
Week 7
Apr 26		Free-response practice + Practice Exam 2Redo every question you missed on Practice Exam 1. Then 3 free-response questions per session, mixing the calculator-required Part A type with the no-calculator Part B type, since four of the six free-response questions allow no calculator. Take Practice Exam 2 at the end of the week and compare it with Practice Exam 1 by unit.
Week 8
May 3		Sharpen & restShort spaced review across all 8 units. 2 free-response questions per day Mon-Thu. Fri-Sun: flashcards and light review only. The exam is Monday morning, May 10.

Unit weights on the AP Calc AB exam

The CED publishes these AB weights for the multiple-choice section. Use them to give more review time to higher-weighted units if you are short on time.

  • Unit 1: Limits and Continuity: 10-15%
  • Unit 2: Differentiation: Definition and Fundamental Properties: 10-15%

The 4-week compressed version

If you have four weeks or less until the exam, compress the plan like this. You will lose depth on the lowest-weighted units. That is a fair trade for exposure to every unit before test day.

  • Week 1: Units 1, 2, 3 (limits and derivatives). Skim, do 15 multiple-choice questions per unit.
  • Week 2: Units 4, 5. Two timed free-response questions. Practice Exam 1.
  • Week 3: Units 6, 7, 8 (integrals and applications), with at least two sessions on Unit 6. Practice Exam 2 at the end of the week.
  • Week 4: Weak-area targeting Mon-Wed. Light review until the exam.

What most AP Calc AB students get wrong

They practice everything with a calculator. Part A of the multiple-choice section (29 of the 42 questions) and Part B of the free-response section (4 of the 6 questions) do not allow a graphing calculator. Do most of your practice by hand, and save the calculator for the sections that require it.

They study from old practice exams without noticing the format. Some older released exams use a different number of multiple-choice questions and different section timings. The calculus carries over, but pace yourself to the current format: 29 questions in 62 minutes, then 13 in 38 minutes.

They save all free-response practice for the last two weeks. By then, they are learning the format under time pressure. Start in week 2.
