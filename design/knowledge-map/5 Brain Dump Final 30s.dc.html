Phospholipid bilayer.

The bilayer is made of phospholipids, each one has a hydrophilic phosphate head and two hydrophobic fatty acid tails. In water they self assemble so the tails face inward and the heads face out toward the cytosol and the extracellular fluid. That makes it a selectively permeable barrier: small nonpolar molecules like O2 and CO2 diffuse straight through, but ions and glucose need transport proteins.



Cholesterol sits between the tails and buffers fluidity, it keeps the membrane from getting too rigid when cold and too loose when warm. Unsaturated tails have kinks so they pack less tightly, which raises fluidity. Membrane proteins are integral, spanning the whole bilayer, or peripheral, sitting on one face. Glycolipids and glycoproteins on the outer face handle cell recognition.



The fluid mosaic model is the name for all of this: the lipids and proteins drift laterally, they do not flip across the bilayer on their own, flippases do that
