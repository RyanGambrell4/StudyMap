1 · Plan math

Each session has a scheduled date, a duration (minutes), and a done flag.
done = count(sessions where done)
remaining = total − done
hoursRemaining = Σ remaining (duration_i) / 60 — shown to 2 significant decimals (9.0, 5.25, 6.0).
expectedByToday = count(sessions where scheduledDate ≤ today) — a session counts as expected the moment its scheduled date has passed, whether or not it was done.
behind = max(0, expectedByToday − done) — the behind state renders iff behind ≥ 1.
stillScheduled = remaining − behind — sessions whose date has not yet passed.
daysToExam = examDate − today (calendar days, ceil).
Progress bar segments (widths = count / total as %): done blue → behind amber (behind state only) → stillScheduled light blue. Segments always sum to total.
Worked example (3c): total 12 at 45 min, done 4, expectedByToday 7 → behind 3, remaining 8, stillScheduled 5, hoursRemaining 6.0, daysToExam 9. Bar 33.3% + 25% + 41.7%. The copy's "7 sessions expected" and "8 sessions left" appear only in the amber block; 4, 6.0, and 9 appear only in the footer stats; 3 appears only in the bar legend and amber label.
Fresh (3a): done 0, bar is one light-blue track. Mid (3b): 5 done, 5.25 hrs. Complete (3d): bar fully blue, legend "Done 12 of 12", footer swaps Sessions/Hours remaining for Hours studied 9.0.

2 · Catch-up rule

"Catch up" rebuilds the schedule for the remaining sessions across the days from tomorrow to the day before the exam:
1. Redistribute remaining sessions evenly across available days, at most one session per day while remaining ≤ daysLeft.
2. If remaining > daysLeft, first shorten review sessions (Spaced review type) from 45 to 30 min, then merge adjacent sessions that share a topic theme (e.g. two metabolism sessions become one 60 min session), until sessions fit at max two per day.
3. The mock exam session always stays last, on the final available day.
4. Session content is never dropped, only compressed or merged; the plan re-pushes to the calendar automatically if it was pushed.
Button copy states the effect before it runs; the amber block explains it in one sentence ("reworks the N sessions left to fit the time before the exam, shortening reviews and merging overlapping topics").
